Disease: Asthma and Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)
Category: Respiratory System Disorder

Overview:
Asthma is a chronic inflammatory condition of the airways causing reversible bronchial hyperresponsiveness and episodic airflow obstruction. COPD represents progressive, largely irreversible lung damage characterized by chronic bronchitis and emphysema.

Key Symptoms:
- Wheezing (high-pitched whistling sound during expiration)
- Chronic, persistent cough (dry in asthma; productive with mucus in COPD)
- Shortness of breath (dyspnea) aggravated by physical activity or cold air
- Chest tightness or feeling like an elastic band around the chest
- Frequent nocturnal awakenings due to coughing or breathing difficulty
- Increased fatigue and frequent chest infections

Critical Biomarkers / Lab Indicators:
- Spirometry: Reduced Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 second (FEV1)
- FEV1 / FVC ratio < 0.70 post-bronchodilator confirms persistent airflow limitation
- Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) variability > 10%
- Pulse oximetry showing resting SpO2 < 92% during exacerbations
- Elevated fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) in eosinophilic asthma

Primary Risk Factors:
- Cigarette smoking (primary cause of COPD) or prolonged secondhand smoke exposure
- Atopy or family history of allergic rhinitis, eczema, and asthma
- Environmental exposures: air pollution, chemical fumes, and organic dusts
- Childhood respiratory viral infections (RSV, rhinovirus)
- Cold weather, exercise, strong perfumes, or pollen triggers

Precautions & Clinical Management:
- Immediate cessation of all tobacco smoking and avoidance of vaping
- Consistent compliance with prescribed inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) and long-acting beta-agonists (LABA)
- Keep quick-relief rescue inhaler (albuterol) accessible at all times
- Annual influenza and pneumococcal vaccinations to prevent exacerbations
- Pulmonary rehabilitation and scheduled Spirometry follow-ups with a pulmonologist
