Disease: Migraine Headache and Chronic Tension-Type Headaches
Category: Neurological Disorder

Overview:
Migraine is a neurovascular headache disorder characterized by recurrent attacks of moderate to severe throbbing head pain, typically unilateral, associated with autonomic and sensory symptoms like photophobia, phonophobia, and nausea.

Key Symptoms:
- Unilateral (one-sided) throbbing or pulsating head pain lasting 4 to 72 hours
- Aggravation by routine physical activity (walking, climbing stairs)
- Sensitivity to light (photophobia) and sensitivity to sound (phonophobia)
- Nausea and vomiting during headache episodes
- Sensory aura in ~25-30% of patients: shimmering zigzag lines (scintillating scotoma), blind spots, or unilateral numbness
- Prodrome symptoms: neck stiffness, yawning, food cravings, fatigue occurring 24 hours prior

Critical Biomarkers / Lab Indicators:
- Migraine is primarily diagnosed clinically via International Classification of Headache Disorders (ICHD-3) criteria
- Brain MRI or CT scans rule out secondary causes (intracranial hemorrhage, aneurysm, space-occupying lesions) when red flags ("SNOOP") are present
- Blood tests: Normal ESR/CRP (rules out temporal arteritis in patients > 50 years)

Primary Risk Factors:
- Female sex (migraines are 3x more common in women due to estrogen fluctuations)
- Positive family history of migraines (strong genetic component)
- Environmental & emotional triggers: emotional stress, sleep disturbances, skipping meals
- Dietary triggers: aged cheeses, processed meats with nitrates, MSG, artificial sweeteners, red wine, caffeine withdrawal
- Weather or barometric pressure shifts, sensory overload (flickering bright lights, loud noises)

Precautions & Clinical Management:
- Rest in a quiet, dark, temperature-controlled room during acute attacks; apply cold compress to forehead or neck
- Maintain consistent sleep-wake cycles, balanced meals, and regular hydration (at least 2-3 liters water daily)
- Keep a detailed headache diary logging potential triggers, frequency, and symptom duration
- Acute abortive therapy: Triptans (e.g., sumatriptan) taken early during pain onset, or NSAIDs
- For frequent attacks (> 4 headache days/month), consult a neurologist for preventive therapy (beta-blockers, CGRP antagonists)
