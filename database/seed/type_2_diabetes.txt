Disease: Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus
Category: Endocrine & Metabolic Disorder

Overview:
Type 2 diabetes is a chronic metabolic condition characterized by insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency, leading to chronically elevated blood glucose levels (hyperglycemia).

Key Symptoms:
- Frequent urination (polyuria), particularly at night (nocturia)
- Excessive and unquenchable thirst (polydipsia)
- Persistent hunger (polyphagia) despite normal food intake
- Unexplained weight loss or chronic fatigue
- Blurred vision and slow wound healing
- Frequent infections (e.g., skin, gums, or urinary tract)
- Tingling, pain, or numbness in the hands or feet (peripheral neuropathy)

Critical Biomarkers / Lab Indicators:
- Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) >= 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L)
- Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) >= 6.5%
- Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) 2-hour postprandial glucose >= 200 mg/dL
- Random plasma glucose >= 200 mg/dL accompanied by classic hyperglycemic symptoms

Primary Risk Factors:
- Overweight or obesity (especially abdominal/visceral adiposity)
- Sedentary lifestyle with minimal physical activity
- Age 45 years or older
- First-degree family history of type 2 diabetes
- History of gestational diabetes or polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
- Pre-existing hypertension (>= 140/90 mmHg) or dyslipidemia (low HDL, high triglycerides)

Precautions & Clinical Management:
- Regular blood glucose self-monitoring and quarterly HbA1c tests
- Adopt a balanced, low-glycemic Mediterranean or DASH diet high in dietary fiber
- Engage in at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise per week
- Routine screening for diabetic retinopathy, nephropathy (urine microalbumin), and foot ulcers
- Consult an endocrinologist or primary care physician for oral hypoglycemic agents or insulin therapy
